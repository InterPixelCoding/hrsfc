## Ariane
Ariane represents the pragmatic east German youth, who adapts to the changes and opportunities brought by reunification. Ariane drops out of university, instead to work at Burger King, where she meets her manager, called Rainer, whom she soon starts dating. Her sudden abandonment of academia reflects the stark shift from socialist austerity to Western consumer culture. Ariane genuinely cares for her mother; she goes along with Alex' delusion by dressing in old clothes and finding DDR products.
## Rainer
Ariane’s boyfriend Rainer is a friendly caricature of a West Berliner – he is bemused by East German customs (like when he mistakes the Young Pioneers’ socialist songs for something Nazi-related, a comedic misunderstanding). Through such interactions, the film highlights misunderstandings but also the connection between the two German cultures.
## Lara
Lara meets Alex during the protest march, but they are separated by the Volkspolizei. After Christiane falls into a coma, Alex is reintroduced to her as she is a nurse caring for his mother. Lara is an exchange student from the Soviet Union, so she asserts an outsider's perspective on the societal changes of reunification. She stays honest and compassionate as Alex' girlfriend, but also becomes a moral conscience in the narrative, as she begins to urge Alex to reveal the truth to his Mother. Lara represents the theme of truth and acceptance, juxtaposing the troubled comfort Alex finds in concealing the reality to his mother.
## Denis
Denis meets Alex as a colleague for a west German installation company. Alex soon finds out about his passion for filmmaking, which lead to them creating fabricated news stories that help conceal the profound societal changes present during Christiane's coma. 
## die Nachbarn
The neighbours represent the Ostalgie felt by the primarily middle aged east Germans. They cooperate with Alex' fabricated reality; they must adapt to western culture outside, but maintain their previous eastern lifestyle inside - a symbol of the middle aged struggle to adapt the reunification (too young to retire, but too old to learn new skills).
## Robert Kerner
Robert Kerner is Alex and Ariane's father. His influence on them looms throughout the film, despite him only appearing late in the film. Christiane tells the children that he abandoned them for a mistress in the west, but the truth is later revealed by Christiane at the Dacha as much more complicated. Robert Kerner had to escape east Germany as he was being persecuted by the authorities. Christiane and the children were then meant to reunite with him, but Christiane didn't feel able to leave, as she feared the authorities would take her children. Robert’s story reflects the **opportunities and losses** involved in East-West migration. On one hand, he found freedom and prosperity in the West; on the other hand, he lost decades with his children. The film reunites them in Christiane’s final days. Alex persuades Robert to visit Christiane one last time. Their reunion at Christiane’s bedside is poignant – Christiane apologises for not having followed him, and Robert provides comfort and closure.
## Herr Klapprath
Herr Klapprath plays a small but meaningful role. As a former official connected to the old school system, he represents the lingering presence of the GDR’s bureaucratic culture after reunification. Herr Klapprath dismisses Christiane Kerner from her position after she publicly criticises the state, demonstrating how quickly dissent could lead to professional consequences in the GDR. He complies in helping Alex celebrate an east German birthday party for his mother. Through Klapprath, the film hints at how ordinary individuals participated in, benefited from, and later adapted to the political structures around them.
